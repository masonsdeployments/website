# Rafiqi: Building Egypt's Mental Health Companion

## Why We Started This

As Egyptian engineers, designers, and researchers, we kept seeing the same gap: imported mental health tools fail when they ignore local language, family structure, and economic reality.

In Egypt, 60% of the population is under 30, and many young adults face stress, anxiety, and depression with access to 1 psychiatrist per 200,000 people.

## The Barriers on the Ground

Five structural gaps block access to care:

- **Cost**: Private therapy sessions exceed what most people can afford from a monthly salary.
- **Geography**: Clinical specialists cluster in Cairo and Alexandria, leaving millions outside major cities underserved.
- **Cultural mismatch**: Western therapeutic scripts overlook Egyptian family structures and social expectations.
- **Dialect gap**: Few mental health tools understand Egyptian Arabic idioms and everyday phrasing.
- **Stigma**: Asking for psychological support still carries heavy social friction.

Conversations with friends, classmates, and family members made those numbers concrete. Existing apps failed to meet people in the language they think and speak in.

## Our Approach: Culture First

We named the product **Rafiqi** ("My Companion") around a clear principle: software should adapt to local culture.

### Core Capabilities

**An AI Mental Health Companion That:**

- Converses in Egyptian Arabic, recognizing local idioms and cultural references.
- Accounts for Egyptian family dynamics and social expectations.
- Respects religious habits and daily prayer routines.
- Delivers 24/7 support at a fraction of clinic therapy costs.
- Retains core offline access for regions with unreliable connectivity.

### Technical Architecture

We designed Rafiqi from scratch around Egyptian speech patterns and clinical safety protocols instead of translating a generic English therapy bot.

**Core Features:**

- **Dialect-Aware Companion**: Trained on Egyptian conversational corpora and cultural contexts.
- **Mood Tracking**: Check-in scales written around how Egyptians describe emotional states.
- **Personal Wellness Plans**: Routines structured around prayer times, work hours, and family schedules.
- **Crisis Escalation**: Connected to local Egyptian emergency hotlines and escalation rules.
- **Guided Mindfulness**: Combines Islamic reflection practices with evidence-based breathing and grounding exercises.

## Technical Challenges

### Challenge 1: Cultural Nuance in Language

**The Problem**: Distinguishing everyday Egyptian hyperbole and frustration from genuine emotional distress.

**Our Approach**: We collaborate with Egyptian linguists and cultural advisors to build annotated dialect datasets so the model separates idioms from risk signals.

### Challenge 2: Crisis Detection in Local Context

**The Problem**: Risk classifiers tuned on Western phrasing can miss or misclassify Egyptian expressions of crisis.

**Our Solution**: We tune risk detection models on Egyptian phrasing while keeping strict recall thresholds for real emergencies.

### Challenge 3: Family-Centered Support

**The Problem**: Conventional apps assume an isolated individual user, whereas emotional health in Egypt intertwines with family and community.

**Our Design**: Users keep strict personal privacy by default while gaining optional controls to share selected progress or involve trusted family members.

## Core Principles

- **Accessibility**: Mental health support must reach beyond high-income neighborhoods.
- **Cultural Respect**: Digital tools should work alongside community and spiritual practices.
- **Local Proof First**: Solving the Egyptian context builds a grounded foundation before expanding across the region.
- **Transferable Blueprint**: Lessons learned in Egyptian Arabic inform future dialect-first health products.

## Implementation Roadmap

### Phase 1: Foundation

We started with OpenAI GPT models and tuned prompts, evaluation suites, and safety layers for Egyptian Arabic:

- Built conversational datasets in Egyptian dialects.
- Assembled cultural context reference guides.
- Reviewed sample dialogues with licensed Egyptian mental health professionals.
- Mapped crisis escalation steps to local emergency services.

### Phase 2: Community Integration

We pair engineering with on-the-ground partnerships:

- Partnering with Egyptian universities and student communities.
- Collaborating with licensed local therapists for clinical review.
- Mapping referral paths into existing clinics where available.
- Training community health volunteers to guide first-time users.

### Phase 3: Scaling Across Egypt

Our rollout targets 27 Egyptian governorates, adapting for:

- Regional dialect variations outside Cairo.
- Varying smartphone specs and digital literacy levels.
- Different income tiers.
- Rural and urban support networks.

## Target Milestones

**Quantitative Targets:**

- 100,000+ active users within the first year.
- 95%+ crisis escalation accuracy rate.
- Coverage across 27 Egyptian governorates within 24 months.
- 70%+ weekly active retention.

**Qualitative Goals:**

- Lower stigma around asking for mental health support.
- Higher mental health literacy among young Egyptians.
- Stronger bridges between trusted community support and clinical care.
- A repeatable reference architecture for dialect-first digital health.

## Lessons Learned

Engineering Rafiqi showed us that model benchmarks mean little if responses sound foreign to the person reading them. Each model change, safety check, and conversation flow goes through one filter: does this help someone in our community right now?
